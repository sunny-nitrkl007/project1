======================================================================
Why the Bridge is needed (and why "just convert one channel at a
time until everything is DDS" doesn't get rid of it)
======================================================================

THE SHORT ANSWER
----------------------------------------------------------------------
It depends on who is on the OTHER end of the channel.

The 3 (really 5) channels that went direct DDS-to-DDS with no Bridge
share one property: both real endpoints are JobMgr and WeighApp --
the two apps actually being converted in this project. Once both
sides speak DDS, the conversion for that channel is simply complete.
There's nothing left to bridge.

Every other channel has an endpoint that is NOT JobMgr or WeighApp --
it's a separate, real component this project was never scoped to
touch. That's the whole reason the Bridge exists, and it's a
permanent architectural seam, not a temporary stepping stone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE CHANNELS THAT DIDN'T NEED A BRIDGE, AND WHY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
LpsSaWeighReqstChannel, LpsSaWeighRespChannel, LpsSaWeighTxChannel,
plus the 2 hybrid legs (LpsSaJobMgrReqstChannel's WeighApp leg,
LpsSaJobMgrTxChannel's direct leg) -- all 5 are JobMgr<->WeighApp
only. No third party ever reads or writes them. Converting both
apps' code for these channels IS the whole job; there's no gap left
for anything to fall into.

This is why Stage 1 could pick exactly these channels for a minimal
first slice: they're the only channels where "convert it and you're
done" is actually true in isolation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CHANNELS THAT DO NEED A BRIDGE, AND WHY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Every other real channel has a second party outside this project's
scope:

  - SwitchInputScs       -> the real switch/button hardware (or
                             whatever driver actually reads it)
  - OutputChannel         -> real GPIO output hardware (drives the
                             horn, etc.)
  - LpsSaJobMgrReqstChannel's OTHER leg -> the legacy UI app
                             (AisJhm2RequestProcessor), explicitly
                             still SCS-only, not part of this project
  - DisplayState          -> published by a separate DisplayApp
  - ShmClock              -> a separate system/hardware clock source
  - EventDiagnosticData   -> hardware/ECM diagnostics, a separate
                             source
  - AutonomyConditionDiagnosticsTxChannel -> a separate autonomy
                             component

None of these are being converted here. Some of them plausibly
CAN'T be -- a GPIO board or a switch driver almost certainly isn't
something you put ROS2/DDS on. Converting JobMgr's side of
SwitchInputScs to DDS doesn't make the physical switch start
speaking DDS. The other end just... doesn't move.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE PART THAT MAKES "GRADUAL, NO BRIDGE" IMPOSSIBLE, NOT JUST
   INCONVENIENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
This isn't only a scope argument -- it's a protocol one. Once
JobMgr's object for a channel is a DDS wrapper
(ros2_wrapper::RosInputInterface<T>/RosOutputInterface<T>), it
literally cannot talk to a real SCS InputInterface<T>/
OutputInterface<T> anymore. Different transport, different wire
format, not interoperable at any level.

So the instant JobMgr's side of SwitchInputScs converts, the channel
goes dead UNLESS something in the middle holds a real SCS handle on
one side and a DDS wrapper on the other, and actively relays between
them. That's not an optional nicety -- it's the only way the channel
keeps working at all once one side has moved and the other hasn't.
That "something in the middle" is the Bridge.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW THE BRIDGE ACTUALLY WORKS, MECHANICALLY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For each such channel-leg, the Bridge constructs BOTH a real SCS
handle (task::InterfaceDb::fetch/bind -- the exact same mechanism
the original, pre-migration JobMgr/WeighApp code used) and a DDS
wrapper on the same topic name/type JobMgr or WeighApp already uses.
Once per tick, it drains one side and republishes onto the other:

  Publisher relay (JobMgr/WeighApp -> legacy peer):
    read the DDS topic JobMgr/WeighApp already publishes to
      -> write the real SCS output object

  Subscriber relay (legacy peer -> JobMgr/WeighApp):
    read the real SCS input object
      -> republish onto the same DDS topic JobMgr/WeighApp already
         subscribes to

From JobMgr/WeighApp's point of view, nothing distinguishes a
Bridge-relayed channel from a directly-wired one -- same topic name,
same message type, same get()/publish() calls. The Bridge is
invisible to them; it only matters to whatever's still SCS-only on
the other side.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. IS IT PERMANENT, OR DOES IT GO AWAY EVENTUALLY?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Per-leg, it's permanent for exactly as long as that leg's other
party stays SCS-only -- which, given those parties are outside this
project's scope, could be indefinitely, or could change if some
separate future project converts the legacy UI or the hardware
drivers too. If that ever happens, that specific leg's Bridge relay
becomes removable (the two sides could go direct DDS-to-DDS, same as
the 5 legs that never needed it) -- but that's a decision for
whoever owns that other component, not something this migration
controls or should assume will happen.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHY ONE SHARED BRIDGE PROCESS, NOT ONE PER CHANNEL OR ONE PER APP
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A per-app bridge (one JobMgr-facing relay process, one WeighApp-
facing one) was considered and rejected in favor of a single shared
process. Two reasons: it matches the "one boundary, one seam"
framing -- there's conceptually one line between "migrated" and
"not yet migrated," so one process modeling that line is cleaner
than several -- and its channel list is a visible, incrementally-
growable measure of how much of the system has actually been
migrated so far. Every leg added to the Bridge is a leg that's been
verified and wired; the list itself is a progress record.
